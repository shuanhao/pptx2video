## 9. Known Mechanism Analysis (Supplementary)

**Colon handling.** All 4 items contain at least one colon (CJK `：` or
ASCII `:`): slide2 (1), slide3 (4), slide4 (4), smoke (2) — 11 occurrences
total across the sample. Direct comparison of each item's divergent cut
positions against colon locations found **genuine colon-driven divergent
cuts in 3 of the 4 items** (slide3 position 221, right after "第二行：";
slide4 positions 52 and 476, right after "例如：" and "提醒："; smoke
positions 88, 319–320, right after "問：" and "故事："). In every located
instance, production cuts immediately after the colon and the adapter does
not — the same mechanism already documented and accepted in the prior
review. None of these are the item's *first* divergence (word-boundary or
DP-optimization divergences occur earlier in every item), so they do not
appear in §7's category counts, but they are real and frequent in this
sample: colon-adjacent divergences occurred roughly once per 275
characters of input in the 3 affected items. This does not change the
prior review's classification of colon-handling as ACCEPTED, but it does
provide the first concrete evidence that colon divergences are not rare
in longer, multi-sentence narration text — they were essentially certain
to occur at least once whenever a colon appeared in this sample.

**Raw vs. normalized width (whitespace-run mechanism).** Zero of the 4
items contain a repeated-whitespace run (`[ \t]{2,}`, checked directly by
regex over all 4 raw texts — none matched). This specific mechanism
(over-eager cuts caused by raw, un-normalized width measurement inflating
literal whitespace) was **not exercised at all** by this sample. This
sample cannot confirm or refute how common multi-space runs are in real
project notes generally (typing/paste artifacts were the synthetic
corpus's own stated rationale for including this case, per the prior
review — this sample simply doesn't happen to contain any).

**Closing delimiters.** Zero of the 4 items contain any of the searched
patterns (`？」`, `！」`, `。」`, standalone `」`/`』`/`）`/`)` immediately
after sentence-final punctuation) — checked by direct regex over all 4
raw texts, zero matches. The previously identified adjacent-`SENTENCE_FINAL`
mechanism (§8 of the prior review, X06) is **not exercised at all** by
this sample.

**CJK word-boundary divergence.** Occurred as the *first* divergence in
2 of 4 items (50%) — a materially higher rate than the synthetic corpus's
1-in-15 rate, though this sample is far too small to treat that
comparison as meaningful. Both concrete instances (slide2's "變成", smoke's
"第三季") were confirmed via direct `jieba.cut()` tokenization to be real,
complete words/phrases that the adapter's cut splits and production's
cut does not. In both cases this is the same shared blind spot already
documented in the prior review (§11 there) — neither system has
word-segmentation awareness, and production's avoidance in these two
instances is, as before, a byproduct of its own greedy per-piece packing
happening to land elsewhere, not a deliberate word-boundary policy
(confirmed again by inspecting production's own algorithm docstring,
`src/subtitle_segmenter.py` lines 52–66: production packs by
sentence/clause-punctuation pieces, never by word).

**Short input.** None of the 4 items are short — lengths are 454, 501,
536, and 417 characters. The 0–1 character contract difference (prior
review §9) is not exercised or evaluated by this sample at all; this
sample provides no evidence either way for its real-world frequency.

## 10. Representative Real-Content Examples

**Word-boundary split — slide2 ("變成"):** production's segment 3 is
`真的有可能把簡報內容` (offsets 34–44); segment 4 is
`變成一段很自然的演講稿` (44–56). The adapter instead cuts at 45: segment 3
is `真的有可能把簡報內容變` (34–45), segment 4 is `成一段很自然的演講稿`
(45–56) — splitting `變成` ("to become") into `變` at the end of one line
and `成` at the start of the next. Confirmed via `jieba.cut()`: `變成` is
tokenized as one token in this context. Mechanism:
WORD_BOUNDARY_DIVERGENCE, no jieba awareness anywhere upstream of
Phase 2D. Impact: Low.

**Word-boundary split — smoke test ("第三季"):** production's segment 1 is
`這份簡報要介紹的是我們在2026年` (0–17); segment 2 is
`第三季推出的新產品「智慧助理Pro」` (17–36). The adapter cuts at 18:
segment 1 is `這份簡報要介紹的是我們在2026年第` (0–18), segment 2 is
`三季推出的新產品「智慧助理Pro」` (18–36) — splitting `第三季` ("third
quarter", a real 3-character jieba token) into `第` at the end of the
first line and `三季` at the start of the second. Mechanism:
WORD_BOUNDARY_DIVERGENCE. Impact: Medium (a named, meaning-bearing time
reference broken mid-word, at the opening line of the narration).

**DP-global-optimization bypassing a scored clause boundary — slide3:**
production's first segment is `這一頁是另一個較長的示範` (0–13), cutting
immediately after the comma at position 12 — a position independently
confirmed, by directly inspecting Phase 2C's own scored output for this
text, to be a genuine `BoundaryClass.CLAUSE`, score 35.0 candidate. The
adapter's first segment instead runs to `這一頁是另一個較長的示範，內容會包含`
(0–18), passing straight over that score-35 candidate to land on
position 18 — confirmed by the same inspection to be a plain
`BoundaryClass.OTHER`, score 0.0 candidate with no clause, colon,
whitespace, or word-boundary evidence attached. Mechanism: Phase 2D
computes a single width-feasible minimum segment count `K` for the *entire
remaining paragraph* (not per natural clause-piece, unlike production's
own per-piece greedy packer, `src/subtitle_segmenter.py` lines 60–66); the
DP then selects, among all `K`-feasible partitions, the one maximizing
total score, tie-broken by lowest sum-of-squared-widths. When including
position 13 as a cut would require more than the paragraph-wide minimum
`K`, no `K`-feasible solution can include it, regardless of its own local
score — it is excluded from consideration entirely, not "outscored."
Production, by contrast, always takes the first available clause-piece
break once the next piece can no longer fit, with no paragraph-wide
lookahead. Mechanism family: the same DP-vs-greedy divergence already
named in the prior review (§7 there, "mechanism (i)"), but this is the
first time it has been observed to *bypass* an available, well-scored
clause boundary rather than merely extending across multiple already-
sentence-delimited clauses (as in the synthetic corpus's H2 case).
Classified `OTHER_UNCLASSIFIED` by the existing harness — see §11 for why.
Impact: Medium.

**Same mechanism — slide4:** production cuts at position 11 (right after
the comma at position 10, confirmed `CLAUSE`, score 35.0); the adapter
extends to position 16 (confirmed plain `OTHER`, score 0.0). Same
mechanism and classification as slide3.

**Colon-driven divergence — smoke test:** production's segment ends at
offset 88, immediately after `：` in `大家可能會問：`; the adapter's
corresponding segment instead continues past the colon into
`大家可能會問：這樣的`, cutting later at offset 91. Same, already-documented
mechanism as the prior review's items C/D.

## 11. OTHER_UNCLASSIFIED Review

Two items (slide3, slide4) classified `OTHER_UNCLASSIFIED`. Both were
investigated at the source level using the existing (unmodified)
`_first_divergent_window`/`_has_colon_evidence`/`_has_word_boundary_evidence`/
`_has_width_measurement_evidence` helpers, plus direct inspection of the
real `WeightedBoundary` scores Phase 2C produced for each text (read-only;
no scoring or classification code was modified to do this).

**slide3.** Divergence window: `(0, 18)`. `_has_colon_evidence`: False.
`_has_word_boundary_evidence`: False (the adapter's actual cut, position
18, sits between the jieba tokens `包含` and `更` — a real word boundary,
not a mid-word split). `_has_width_measurement_evidence`: False.

**slide4.** Divergence window: `(0, 16)`. Same three checks: all False.

**Why the classifier found no evidence, despite a real, locatable
mechanism existing (§10):** both windows share the pattern "production's
segment and the adapter's segment start at the same offset (0) but end at
different offsets." `_first_divergent_window()` computes the window as
`(min(starts), max(ends))` — here `(0, 18)` and `(0, 16)` respectively —
and `classify_divergence()` then checks evidence only at exactly
`w_start` and `w_end` (`cut_positions = (w_start, w_end)`), **not** at
production's own actual, shorter cut offset (13 for slide3, 11 for
slide4). Since `text[12] == '，'` and `text[10] == '，'` respectively — both
members of `_TRAILING_STRIP_CHARS` — genuine width/clause-adjacency
evidence *does* exist in both texts, immediately before production's real
cut point, but that specific offset (13 / 11) is never included in
`cut_positions` when the two segments' start offsets coincide and only
their end offsets differ. This is a **structural blind spot in the
existing evidence-window construction**, not a bug in the underlying
Phase 1–2D pipeline, and not something this evaluation modifies (per the
explicit scope boundary — `test_shadow_mode_comparison.py` was not
touched). It is reported here, in detail, precisely because the task
requires investigating every `OTHER_UNCLASSIFIED` case rather than
accepting the label at face value.

Both items' true underlying mechanism, based on this investigation, is
the same paragraph-wide DP-vs-greedy-packing family already named and
accepted in the prior review (§7 there) — not a new production-behavior
mechanism. What is new is the discovery of the classifier's blind spot for
this particular divergence shape (shared start, differing end, with the
evidence-bearing position at the *shorter* segment's own boundary rather
than at either window bound).

## 13. Corpus Coverage / Limitations

This supplementary sample can establish:

- That every one of the four already-documented divergence mechanisms
  (colon, DP-vs-greedy, word-boundary) is reproducible on
  narration-shaped, multi-paragraph, multi-sentence text — not just on
  the synthetic corpus's short, purpose-built probes.
- That a single real multi-paragraph narration item commonly exercises
  *more than one* divergence mechanism internally (§7's caveat) — a
  finding the 15-item synthetic corpus's one-mechanism-per-item design
  could not surface.
- A concrete example of a genuine classifier evidence-window blind spot
  (§11) that the synthetic corpus's item shapes never happened to trigger.

This supplementary sample **cannot** establish:

- What fraction of real project slide content diverges from production
  (4 items, all diverging, is far too small and non-random a sample to
  generalize a rate from — and both items are developer-authored test
  text, not real usage).
- How common whitespace-run artifacts (typed/pasted double-spaces) or
  closing-delimiter patterns are in real slide notes — neither mechanism
  is exercised anywhere in this sample.
- Whether the 0–1 character short-input edge case ever occurs in real
  slide content.
- Whether any High-impact divergence exists in real usage — none appeared
  in this sample, but 4 items is not enough to rule it out.

The evidence gap identified by the prior review (§16 item 5 there) —
"a sample of real project slide notes... should be run through both
systems to establish an actual divergence rate" — **remains open**. This
document's supplementary observation is a lower-confidence proxy, not a
resolution of that gap.

## 14. Production Impact Assessment

Taken at face value and clearly bounded by everything in §13, this
supplementary sample suggests two things worth carrying forward without
treating either as settled:

1. On text shaped like natural, multi-paragraph, multi-sentence narration
   (as opposed to short single-mechanism probes), Phase 2D's adapter
   consistently produced **fewer, more tightly packed segments** than
   production on all 4 items (average −4, up to −6), while never
   exceeding the width ceiling. If this pattern held on real content, it
   would mean cutover could plausibly *reduce* the number of subtitle
   lines shown per slide, not increase it — the opposite direction from
   the synthetic corpus's whitespace-driven cases, where the adapter
   produced *more* segments. Both directions are real and mechanism-
   distinct (packing efficiency vs. raw-width inflation); this sample is
   the first evidence that the net effect on segment count is not
   one-directional.
2. Word-boundary and colon divergences both occurred readily on this
   sample's longer, more naturalistic text (word-boundary: 2/4 items as
   first divergence; colon: 3/4 items containing at least one instance
   somewhere in the item). Neither produced a High-impact outcome in this
   sample, but both mechanisms are evidently not rare once real
   sentence-length, multi-clause text is involved, which is a more
   concrete data point than the prior review had for either mechanism's
   real-world frequency.

Neither point is a cutover decision. Both are inputs for whoever makes
that decision, clearly labeled by their evidentiary weakness (§13).

This repository does not contain a corpus of real, non-synthetic slide
content. The two candidates found and evaluated as a disclosed
supplementary observation (§6–§10) usefully demonstrate that all three
previously-documented divergence mechanisms (colon, DP-vs-greedy,
word-boundary) reproduce on more naturalistic, multi-paragraph text and
surfaced one genuine classifier coverage gap (§11) worth carrying forward
as an implementation note for whoever eventually touches the classifier —
but neither candidate is real project content, and this document does not
claim otherwise. The specific evidence gap the prior review flagged (§16
item 5 there: an actual divergence rate on real production slide content)
remains open. Real `.pptx` decks or extracted-notes text from actual past
or current use of this tool — not further hand-authored samples — are
needed before that gap can be closed and a cutover decision can be
responsibly designed.

This document does not choose "READY FOR PRODUCTION CUTOVER" — that
determination is out of scope for this round regardless of the evidence
state.
